Terminal Burn / Field guide

Economy

Metal and organic production chains, industrial inputs, and interplanetary supply networks.

Design guide: these chains describe the intended economy. Exact recipes, yields, and building availability require confirmation against the game.

Metal production

Geology determines the feedstock; processing turns it into useful construction and machinery. Concentrated ores should make some sites much more valuable than ordinary crust.

  1. Extraction: mine ore or bulk rock from suitable provinces and deposits.
  2. Beneficiation: separate useful minerals and concentrate the feedstock.
  3. Refining: use process-specific heat, electricity, and reagents to produce metals.
  4. Manufacturing: turn metals into alloys, fittings, machinery, and aerospace parts.

Iron, aluminum, copper, and specialty metals serve different roles. A colony with abundant structural material may still need imports of alloying elements, electronics, or processing chemicals.

Organic production

The proposed organic economy connects carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen feedstocks to food, fertilizers, and polymers. Carbon dioxide, water, nitrogen, ammonia, and outer-system ices offer different starting points; turning them into products requires energy and appropriate processing.

ChainRole
Water → hydrogen; carbon feedstock → chemical intermediatesInputs for synthetic organic chemistry.
Nitrogen + hydrogen → ammoniaLinks fertilizer production with nitrogen-bearing chemical industries.
Chemical intermediates → polymersPlastic goods, synthetic fabrics, gaskets, seals, and composite resins.
Carbon + water + nutrients + energy → biomass → foodAlgae, hydroponic crops, and proposed alternative food production.
Biowaste → recovered nutrients → fertilizerRecycling reduces imports, while losses and population growth still require replacement nutrients.

Fibers and composites

Carbon fiber and polymer resins can support advanced composite products. Glass and basalt fibers provide additional material paths. These industries connect mining and high-temperature processing to the chemical economy.

Why trade?

Different worlds should offer different combinations of concentrated metals, volatiles, energy, and accessible terrain. The intended result is a network of specialized colonies: a rich mining world may depend on imported nitrogen, while an icy outpost supplies chemicals to an industrial center.
